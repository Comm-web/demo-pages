Automatic vs. Semi-Automatic Capsule Filling Machines: How to Choose

◷ 2026/09/29

In pharmaceutical and nutraceutical manufacturing, the right capsule filling machine depends on more than its automation level. Production volume, powder characteristics, batch size, changeover frequency, operator availability, available space, and future plans all affect the decision.

A semi-automatic machine can be a practical choice for smaller or more varied production runs. An automatic machine may be a better fit when demand is stable, output requirements are higher, and the process can run consistently. Neither option is right for every factory.

This guide focuses on capsule filling machines and the production factors manufacturers should compare before choosing a system.

1. Automatic vs. Semi-Automatic: What Changes in the Workflow?

A semi-automatic capsule filling machine combines automated machine functions with operator participation. Depending on the model and production process, operators may need to load materials, handle capsules, monitor filling, or assist with other steps.

An automatic capsule filling machine can automate more of the process, such as empty-capsule feeding, capsule orientation, powder dosing, capsule closing, and discharge. Operators still need to monitor the machine, replenish materials, manage changeovers, and respond to production issues.

The practical difference is how much of the production cycle is automated and how much operator time each batch requires. Compare the actual workflow for your products rather than relying on the machine label alone.

3. Match Capacity to Required Output

Start by calculating the number of acceptable capsules your factory needs to produce per shift or per day. Include operating hours, scheduled cleaning, product changeovers, maintenance, and expected downtime.

Rated capacity is useful for an initial comparison, but it is not the same as guaranteed production output. Actual results depend on the formulation, capsule size, fill weight, machine setup, operator procedures, and production conditions. A machine trial with representative materials can provide a more useful basis for planning.

For reference, Chin Yi lists the ACF-70 Automatic Capsule Filling Machine at a rated capacity of 48,000–52,000 capsules per hour and the ACF-180 Automatic Capsule Filling Machine at 96,000–120,000 capsules per hour. These are model-specific manufacturer ratings. Ask for a current specification and confirm expected output for your product and configuration before making a purchase decision.

When reviewing capacity, ask:

  • What is the required good output per hour, shift, and day?
  • How many hours will the machine run?
  • How much time is needed for cleaning and changeovers?
  • What is the expected reject rate?
  • Is the capsule filling machine the main production bottleneck?
  • How will the machine connect with inspection, polishing, counting, or packaging equipment?

Choosing the highest rated speed does not automatically deliver the best production result. The selected capacity should match your sustained demand and the rest of the line.

4. Confirm Compatibility with the Product and Capsule

Powder flow, density, particle size, and other material characteristics can affect feeding and filling. Manufacturers should evaluate these properties alongside the target fill weight and capsule size.

Chin Yi’s ACF series uses a tamping filling system with a dosing plate and tamping plungers. Selected models can also be configured with pellet-filling equipment. Review the ACF-70 product information and confirm which configuration is suitable for your product.

Before selecting a machine, prepare representative samples and confirm:

  • Capsule size and capsule type
  • Powder, granule, or pellet characteristics
  • Target fill weight and acceptable variation
  • Required feeding and dosing method
  • Product changeover and cleaning requirements
  • Any optional equipment needed for the product

A machine that matches the capsule format but does not handle the material reliably may not meet the production requirement. Ask the supplier to review the product and test it under conditions representative of your process.

5. Consider Batch Size and Changeover Frequency

Batch size affects how much time production spends running versus preparing for the next product. A factory with frequent product changes should measure the time required for setup, adjustment, cleaning, and capsule-size changes.

A semi-automatic machine may be worth evaluating when production runs are small or product variety is high. An automatic machine can be a strong fit when batches are larger and specifications remain stable. However, automation does not automatically mean faster changeovers. The time depends on the machine design, change parts, cleaning procedure, and operator training.

Track the real changeover time for each product. If production loses a large share of each shift to cleaning and setup, a higher rated capacity alone may not solve the problem.

6. Compare Labor and Total Cost of Ownership

The purchase price is only one part of the equipment cost. Compare the full operating cost over the period your factory expects to use the machine.

Include:

  • Machine, tooling, and installation costs
  • Number of operators and operator time per shift
  • Training and changeover time
  • Maintenance, spare parts, and consumables
  • Downtime and product loss
  • Cleaning and inspection requirements
  • Expected production volume and useful equipment life

A semi-automatic machine may have a lower initial cost, but require more operator time per batch. An automatic machine may need a larger upfront investment, while reducing some repetitive handling at higher, stable production volumes.

A useful comparison is the cost per saleable capsule under your expected production schedule. Use your own labor, operating hours, material, and maintenance estimates rather than assuming that automation will always produce a specific return.

7. Check the Production Space and Line Layout

Confirm the machine footprint and the space required to operate, clean, and service it. The layout should also account for material feeding, finished-product discharge, operator access, and connections to other equipment.

For example, Chin Yi lists the ACF-180 at approximately 1,180 × 1,390 × 2,092 mm and 1,930 kg. Confirm current dimensions and transport requirements for the selected configuration before finalizing the factory layout.

Check the complete route from delivery to installation, including:

  • Doorways, elevators, and transport paths
  • Floor capacity and installation position
  • Operator and maintenance access
  • Material and finished-product movement
  • Electrical and other utility requirements
  • Connections to inspection, polishing, counting, or packaging equipment

Planning the complete layout before purchase can help prevent installation delays and workflow problems.

8. Review Cleaning, Operation, and Documentation

For pharmaceutical production, ask how the equipment will fit the factory’s cleaning, operating, and documentation procedures. Requirements can vary by product, facility, and quality system, so confirm them with your internal team before selecting a configuration.

During supplier discussions, review:

  • Access to product-contact parts for cleaning
  • Change parts and tools required for each product
  • Operator training and routine maintenance
  • Safety features and operating controls
  • Available documentation for the selected model
  • Installation, acceptance, and service support

Chin Yi lists IQ/OQ documents as available for the ACF-70. Confirm the documentation scope for the specific model and configuration under consideration.

9. A Practical Decision Checklist

A semi-automatic capsule filling machine may be a suitable option when:

  • Production demand is modest or still developing
  • Product variety or batch changes are frequent
  • Operators are available to take part in the production process
  • The factory wants to limit its initial equipment investment

An automatic capsule filling machine may be a suitable option when:

  • Production demand is stable and sustained
  • The process repeats the same product specifications
  • Higher output or reduced manual handling is a priority
  • The factory can support the equipment, layout, and integration requirements

Use these points to shortlist equipment, then confirm the choice with current specifications and a product trial. If demand is uncertain, compare the cost and operating requirements of each option against a realistic production plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an automatic capsule filling machine always more accurate?

No. Automation can help make a well-set process more repeatable, but filling performance also depends on the formulation, capsule size, dosing method, machine setup, maintenance, and process controls. Test representative product samples and agree on acceptance criteria before choosing a machine.

Should I choose a machine based on its rated capsules-per-hour figure?

Use rated capacity as an initial comparison. For planning, compare good output over a full shift and allow for cleaning, changeovers, replenishment, downtime, and rejects. Ask the supplier to confirm expected performance using your product and configuration.

Can one capsule filling machine handle different products or capsule sizes?

That depends on the machine configuration and the products. Confirm compatible capsule sizes, dosing requirements, change parts, cleaning procedures, and the time required for each product changeover. A product trial can help identify limitations before purchase.

When should I consider a semi-automatic capsule filling machine?

Consider one when production volumes are modest, demand is still developing, batches are small, or operators can take part in the filling workflow. Compare the total cost and operator time with an automatic option against your expected production schedule.

What information should I prepare before requesting a machine quotation?

Share the capsule size, product form and material characteristics, target fill weight, required good output per shift, batch sizes, changeover frequency, available floor space, downstream equipment, and any cleaning or documentation requirements. These details help the supplier assess a suitable machine and configuration.

Conclusion: Choose the Machine That Fits Your Process

The right choice is not simply the machine with the highest level of automation. Compare sustained output, product compatibility, batch size, changeover time, labor, total operating cost, space, and documentation.
